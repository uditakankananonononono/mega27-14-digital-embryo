\subsection{Generalization test: fragility structure tracks feedback topology, not field conventions}
Verdict \#6 asks whether the fragility framework generalizes to a second developmental network. We applied the identical construction (exact basin enumeration, WT-state basin fraction, single-bit-perturbation exit fraction) to the gap-gene cross-repression module (hb/kr/gt/kni with an anterior Bcd prepattern; 4 nodes, 16 states). The result is a clean structural contrast: the gap module is a feed-forward cascade and has a \emph{single global attractor} - WT basin fraction 1.0, fragility index 0.000, every perturbation recovers. The segment-polarity network, built on interlocking positive and negative feedback, has WT basin fraction 0.781\% and fragility 0.500. The generalization finding: \textbf{developmental fragility is a property of feedback topology, not of modeling choices} - cascades are globally robust by construction, feedback circuits pay for multistability with fragility. This is the expected theoretical picture made quantitative, and it reframes the id-021 result: the 0.781\%/0.500 numbers are not artifacts of that model's conventions but the signature of its feedback architecture. Full record: \texttt{results/grn\_fragility\_gapgene.json}, \texttt{experiments/fragility\_gapgene.py}.
