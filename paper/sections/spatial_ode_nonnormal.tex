\section{Negative modal rates coexist with transient linear amplification}
The finite-difference Jacobians above have negative eigenvalue real parts, but this does not imply that every disturbance shrinks monotonically. For a fixed linearization $J$, a disturbance follows $\delta x(t)=\exp(tJ)\delta x(0)$. In the raw-concentration Euclidean norm, the largest possible linear amplification at a specified time is the operator norm $\|\exp(tJ)\|_2$. The initial maximum norm-growth rate is the largest eigenvalue of $(J+J^T)/2$, often called the Euclidean logarithmic norm. These calculations use all 132 floating-species coordinates in their archived order, not a selected subnetwork.

A frozen audit evaluated times 0, 1, 10 and 100 on both existing finite-difference matrices without resimulation or parameter changes. Their spectral abscissae remain approximately $-0.0108609$, yet the logarithmic norm is $+5.28349$ in both. Propagator norms are 1.000 at zero, 9.64113 at one, 36.77820 at ten and 6.20614 at one hundred. Cross-step differences are below $5\times10^{-9}$ on this grid. The largest sampled norm occurs at ten; it is not an optimized peak over continuous time. Thus modal decay and substantial transient amplification coexist in these particular numerical linearizations. Negative eigenvalues alone would have missed this distinction.

This is not a nonlinear perturbation experiment, a biological robustness bound, or a conclusion about a physically feasible worst-case disturbance. Concentration coordinates are not species-scaled, and their Euclidean metric depends on coordinate units. A right-singular direction could require decreasing a species that is already near zero; the next subsection checks only its initial endpoint feasibility, not nonlinear evolution. The base endpoint still has a finite residual, and the native Jacobian still contains nonfinite entries. We retain that failure rather than allowing the finite-difference diagnostic to substitute for native derivative certification. Exact input hashes, fixed-grid norms and scope are in \path{results/spatial_ode_nonnormal_audit.json}; code is \path{experiments/spatial_ode_nonnormal_audit.py}.

\subsection{The maximizing direction has a tiny nonnegative amplitude ceiling}
A second frozen geometry audit takes the leading unit right-singular vector of $\exp(10J)$ and examines both signs without clipping negative components. For a sign-oriented direction $v$, the largest endpoint-feasible nonnegative amplitude is $\alpha_{\max}=\min_{i:v_i<0}x_i/(-v_i)$. This is an initial-concentration constraint, not a nonlinear trajectory calculation. The endpoint norm is 3.08029, and its smallest coordinate is approximately $5.68\times10^{-84}$. Every coordinate is retained, including those near zero.

The sign with 82 negative components has an amplitude ceiling approximately $1.49728\times10^{-6}$, or $4.861\times10^{-7}$ of the endpoint norm, on both finite-difference matrices. Its limiting coordinate is a PTC component in cell three; the exact index switches from PTC1 to PTC4 across the two numerical steps. The opposite sign has 49 negative components and a ceiling approximately $4.7\times10^{-79}$, limited by an HH component in cell one whose concentration is near $5.68\times10^{-84}$. Its exact index also switches. These near-tied limiting identities are not robust biological bottleneck assignments.

Thus the displayed unconstrained linear norm maximum does not imply a large feasible disturbance at this endpoint. The less restrictive sign still has a very small initial amplitude ceiling, and the other is dominated by essentially depleted coordinates. This does not exclude feasible transient growth along other directions, prove positivity invariance, or measure finite-amplitude nonlinear amplification. Singular-vector signs are arbitrary; both are reported so a favorable orientation cannot be selected silently. Roundoff and boundary derivative numerics remain relevant. The exact vectors, signs, limiting concentrations and hashes are in \path{results/spatial_ode_direction_feasibility_audit.json}.

\subsection{Two fixed feasible nonlinear directions decay, rather than realize the maximum}
We next perturb every floating concentration by the same positive fractional increase, $\delta x(0)=\epsilon x(0)$, for fixed $\epsilon=0.001$ and $0.01$. These directions differ from the maximizing singular vector. The pinned SBML model continues from the archived time-11,000 endpoint with unchanged parameters, and a paired unperturbed continuation is subtracted from each perturbed trajectory. CVODE uses relative tolerance $10^{-8}$ and absolute tolerance $10^{-14}$ for this audit. Elapsed times are fixed at zero, one, ten and one hundred; no direction or time is selected after seeing outcomes.

For the 0.1\% increase, raw-coordinate deviation gains are 1.000, 0.952813, 0.709338 and 0.116885. For the 1\% increase they are 1.000, 0.952782, 0.708935 and 0.116608. The corresponding linear gains from the smaller-step archived Jacobian are 1.000, 0.952816, 0.709383 and 0.116916 for this shared direction. Relative nonlinear-versus-linear deviation errors at elapsed one hundred are 0.000946 and 0.009443, respectively. The paired baseline drift norm stays below $1.30\times10^{-8}$ on these sampled times and is reported separately, rather than attributed to the perturbation.

These two selected feasible initial disturbances decay on the sampled grid and approximately track the linearization; they do not realize its much larger unconstrained operator maximum. Sampled concentrations remain nonnegative, but this is not a proof of positivity throughout continuous time or across other directions. Near-zero species fall far below the absolute tolerance, so their individual relative accuracy is not certified. Neither the two amplitudes nor the raw Euclidean metric defines a biological robustness test. The native derivative failure and finite base residual remain. Exact paired outcomes, source and matrix hashes are in \path{results/spatial_ode_nonlinear_audit.json}.

\subsection{Tighter tolerances leave the selected aggregate responses nearly unchanged}
A frozen paired replay tightens CVODE to relative tolerance $10^{-10}$ and absolute tolerance $10^{-16}$, holding both amplitudes, the endpoint, parameters, sample times and linear comparator fixed. Across sampled times, maximum gain changes are $1.85\times10^{-7}$ for the 0.1\% disturbance and $3.04\times10^{-8}$ for the 1\% disturbance. Maximum changes in relative linear-error diagnostics are $2.27\times10^{-7}$ and $5.91\times10^{-8}$, respectively. This supplies a second numerical tolerance check for these aggregate responses, not a proof of exact convergence. The near-depleted species remain far below even the tighter absolute tolerance, so their individual relative accuracy is still uncertified. Native Jacobian failure, finite endpoint residual and the absence of a biological robustness test remain. Full tighter outcomes and comparison hashes are in \path{results/spatial_ode_tolerance_audit.json}.
