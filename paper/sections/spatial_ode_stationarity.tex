\subsection{Finite residuals separate replay from numerical stationarity}
A separately frozen continuation uses the unchanged public spatial SBML setting and the same libRoadRunner CVODE tolerances, extending time from 1,100 to 11,000 in steps of five. No parameter, initial condition or update rule is changed. The first 221 samples reproduce the previous imported trajectory exactly. Across all floating species, the maximum absolute derivative is $2.72\times10^{-8}$ at time 1,100 and $2.79\times10^{-9}$ at time 11,000. The latter passes the frozen $10^{-8}$ numerical residual threshold, while the former does not. This is a finite-state numerical criterion, not a biological tolerance or proof of asymptotic stability.

Maximum endpoint mRNA drift between those times is $1.67\times10^{-7}$. The concentration-scaled derivative maximum decreases from 0.01725 to $1.19\times10^{-7}$, using a $10^{-12}$ denominator floor. Relative residuals can be large in almost-zero species, so the prespecified decision uses the absolute residual. No convergence rate, global attractor, parameter robustness or Boolean equivalence is established.

The strict ptc cell ordering is not a stable biological rescue. Cell 2 minus cell 4 is $-1.63\times10^{-11}$ at time 1,100 but $8.67\times10^{-19}$ at time 11,000. The computed argmax changes from cell 4 to cell 2, yet the final difference is at floating-point rounding scale. It must be reported as an effective numerical tie, not evidence of distinct cell-2 dominance. The en, hh and wg argmax cells remain unchanged. Extending the horizon therefore supports small numerical residuals in this selected setting, but does not turn the earlier strict endpoint failure into robust spatial specificity.
