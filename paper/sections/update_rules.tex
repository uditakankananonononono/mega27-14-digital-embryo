\subsection{Update-rule sensitivity: the basin's rarity is partly a timing artifact}
Weakness \#10 of the provided verdict asks whether synchronous updating itself creates artifacts. It does, and quantifying that is a result: under random-order asynchronous updates over the same 2{,}048 initial states, the wg-ON outcome is reached from $11.04\% \pm 0.12\%$ of states (8 replicated schedules) - \textbf{14$\times$ more accessible than the synchronous 0.781\%}. Adding per-step stochastic bit-flip noise $q$ inflates it further (quarter-sample, 4 reps: $q=0.01 \to 4.9\%$, $q=0.05 \to 18.2\%$, $q=0.1 \to 20.5\%$ of state space (6$\times$, 23$\times$, 26$\times$ the synchronous value)). The developmental interpretation (verdict \#8): real cells do not update synchronously, so the apparent knife-edge rarity of the wild-type state overstates its biological fragility; what IS robust across update rules is the \emph{identity} of the controlling axis - the CI/EN nodes dominate fragility under synchronous update and remain the wiring through which all basin-expanding interventions act.
